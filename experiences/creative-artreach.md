# Creative ArtReach

**Science & Robotics Enrichment Lead · 2021–2026**

My time at Creative ArtReach gave me the opportunity to turn my interests in science and robotics into something I could share with my community. What began as helping with existing enrichment classes eventually grew into something much bigger: a new science and robotics program that I proposed, developed, and led.

Through these programs, I introduced students to robotics, brought hands-on science experiments into their classrooms, and developed activities that encouraged them to explore, experiment, and solve problems for themselves.

## Getting Started with Enrichment

I originally joined Creative ArtReach as an enrichment instructor's assistant, helping other instructors prepare and run their classes. Eventually, I was trusted with classes of my own.

My first independent classes were in art. I would visit our Arts Lead's studio, learn the lessons she had prepared, and then bring those activities to the students. Art wasn't necessarily my area of expertise, but it gave me an opportunity to step outside my comfort zone and learn how to teach something new.

That experience helped me become more comfortable leading a classroom, adapting lessons for different groups, and finding ways to keep students engaged.

It also gave me the confidence to propose something much closer to my own interests: a robotics enrichment program.

---

## Bringing Robotics to Creative ArtReach

Robotics had been a passion of mine since childhood, when I participated in FIRST LEGO League. Getting the opportunity to introduce a new generation of students to robotics was especially exciting.

After pitching the idea to the company owner and securing the investment in VEX GO robotics kits, I began developing and teaching robotics lessons across four towns and more than a dozen schools.

![VEX GO robotics kits]({{ '/assets/images/VEX-GO-product.webp' | relative_url }})

I wanted students to have a strong introduction to robotics, so I incorporated VEX GO's educator curriculum alongside activities of my own. Lessons ranged from guided builds and programming exercises to open-ended engineering challenges.

When introducing block-based programming, I wanted everyone to start with the same robot configuration. I guided students through VEX GO's Code Base build, giving them a consistent starting point before they began experimenting with the programming tools.

![VEX GO code]({{ '/assets/images/VEX-GO-code.png' | relative_url }})

Other lessons gave students much more freedom to explore and solve problems creatively through building. I was constantly surprised by their creativity and ingenuity, especially when they came up with solutions I hadn't anticipated.

### MISSION: MARS Summer Camps

Some of my favorite experiences came from the summer camps I helped run through Creative ArtReach. Having more time with the students allowed us to take on larger projects, including full MISSION: MARS-themed camps.

Naturally, our robots became rovers. Students worked toward a final challenge: navigating the Martian surface, collecting soil samples, and returning them to base.

Watching their excitement as their rovers finally completed the mission was incredibly rewarding. They took so much pride in what they had built, and I loved getting to be part of that process.

![Rovers on the move]({{ '/assets/images/mars-camp.jpg' | relative_url }})

## Expanding Into Science Enrichment

Robotics was only part of the program. I also introduced more than 20 different science activities and experiments to the schools we served.

Some of my favorites included lemon volcanoes, recycled lava lamps, and homemade ice cream shakers. We also held an egg drop competition, challenging students to design and test ways to protect their creations from a fall.

I loved seeing students get excited about the science behind what they were doing. Rather than simply explaining a concept, I could give them something to make, test, observe, and learn from.

Science activities were often a *little* messier than robotics. My hands were constantly covered in baking soda, food coloring, or slime! Unfortunately, that means I have almost no pictures of these lessons. Most of what I do have comes from our egg drop competition, where I let students crack their eggs on my head if their designs survived the fall. My coworkers made absolutely sure that moment was immortalized.

## Building and Leading the Program

As the program grew, so did my responsibilities. I took ownership of the work needed to keep it running, from designing curriculum and organizing lessons to sourcing materials and preparing supplies for each project.

I also created reusable lesson materials so other instructors could independently teach the activities, helping maintain consistency as the program expanded. Much like the Arts Lead had originally taught me her lessons before I brought them into the classroom, I could now share my own robotics and science activities with other instructors, helping the program reach even more students.

Eventually, the program became a full-fledged after-school offering, with special enrichment activities every day. I established the role of **Head Science & Robotics Enrichment** to lead the program and oversee its activities.

We followed rotating eight-week schedules, alternating between robotics and science. Each week introduced a new project, giving students something different to look forward to while building on what they had learned.

I traveled to a different school every afternoon to lead these activities, while also maintaining the lesson materials, project plans, and supplies needed to keep everything running.

What started as an idea I pitched to the company owner had grown into a program serving students across four towns and more than a dozen schools.

## Building Tools for the Program

Working so closely with the program also gave me firsthand experience with the organizational challenges of managing supplies and planning activities across multiple locations.

Keeping track of inventory, knowing which materials were available, and figuring out which projects could be run with existing supplies could be difficult. I wanted to make those tasks easier, so I developed a C# Windows Forms application to manage inventory and help plan activities.

That project became the **Creative ArtReach Inventory Manager**, one of my first opportunities to apply my programming skills to a problem I had personally experienced.

## Looking Back

Creative ArtReach was one of the most rewarding experiences I've had. I joined to help with enrichment classes and ended up building and leading programs that brought robotics and science to students across my community.

It taught me how to take an idea from a proposal to something people could actually experience, how to lead and adapt to different groups, and how much of a difference hands-on learning can make.

Most importantly, I loved getting to share something I was passionate about with students who were discovering it for themselves. Seeing their excitement, creativity, and pride in what they accomplished made all the planning and preparation worthwhile.

And, of course, I got very good at hauling an unreasonable amount of equipment all over my little corner of NorCal.
