# About Me

I'm a computer science student, game developer, and lifelong creative who enjoys making things people can interact with, explore, and enjoy.

I've always been drawn to projects that combine creativity with problem-solving. That interest has taken me through programming, game development, digital illustration, robotics, and even tabletop roleplaying. I enjoy taking an idea, figuring out how its pieces fit together, and turning it into something tangible.

I'm currently pursuing a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science at California State University Channel Islands. Most of my personal development work focuses on game development, but I've also enjoyed building practical software, including tools that solve problems I've encountered in my own work.

Outside of programming, I work as a Game Master, creating and running tabletop roleplaying games for groups both online and in person. I've also had opportunities to run games at conventions for publishers such as Kobold Press and Magpie Games. It's another way I get to combine creativity, problem-solving, and collaboration.

## About My Approach

I'm interested in the intersection of technology, creativity, and user experience.

My experiences in computer science, game development, digital illustration, and education have each influenced how I approach development.

Studying art has taught me to think more carefully about visual communication and how people interpret what they see. It's also helped me collaborate with artists during game jams, where being able to communicate ideas and understand someone else's creative process makes a real difference.

Working in education gave me a different perspective. As a Science and Robotics Enrichment Lead, I developed lessons, introduced students to robotics and programming, and created tools to help organize the program. It taught me to consider the people using the systems I build, especially when they're encountering something unfamiliar for the first time.

These experiences have shaped what I enjoy most about development: understanding how something works behind the scenes while also thinking about how it feels to use. Whether I'm building a gameplay mechanic or a desktop application, I want the individual pieces to come together into an experience that feels intuitive.

I especially enjoy collaborative projects. Some of my favorite experiences have come from working with people who approach problems differently than I do. I like seeing how different perspectives can change an idea and help turn it into something none of us would have created on our own.

## Award-Winning Artwork

### First Place — Faculty Choice Award

**Solano Community College Art Show · Spring 2026**

This piece was created for my Digital Illustration coursework and later printed using screen-printing techniques. One of the things I enjoyed most about the process was discovering how much my computer science background helped me understand the science behind the art.

I wanted to use as many colors as I could, layering orange ink onto the white paper first, followed by blue. The two colors are complementary, and where they overlapped, they produced a much darker color. It was fascinating to see how the individual layers combined to create something different from either color on its own.

The process also reminded me of working with binary. Each screen acts almost like a mask: a given area either allows ink through or blocks it. I thought of those areas as `0` and `1`, or `false` and `true`. When an area was open on the first screen, it received orange. When it was open on the second, it received blue. Where both screens allowed ink through, the paper received both layers, creating that darker overlap.

Thinking about the screens this way made the process feel surprisingly familiar. I was essentially working with separate layers of information, each determining where something would or wouldn't appear, and combining them to produce the final image.

It was a fun reminder that problem-solving doesn't belong to just one discipline. Sometimes the way you understand one subject can completely change how you approach another.
